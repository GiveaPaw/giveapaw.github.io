Credits and Licenses¶

The Give a Paw program builds on work that other people shared freely. This page lists every third-party asset used on this site, its author, and the license it is used under. Where a license requires a specific credit string, that string is reproduced here verbatim.

Canine Reference Meshes¶

The interactive socket demos are generated around scanned canine anatomy. Two of the reference bodies come from Sketchfab and are used under Creative Commons licenses.

Chihuahua — small dog reference

Used as the anatomical base for the Chihuahua socket, interface, and attachment sweeps on the nTop Socket Creation page, and for the solid animal overlay in those demos.

License: CC-BY-4.0 — author must be credited, commercial use allowed.

This work is based on "Chihuahua" (https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/chihuahua-63d89ce1b00f445b926668d19f559a76) by kenchoo (https://sketchfab.com/kenchoo) licensed under CC-BY-4.0 (http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

Beagle — medium dog reference

Used as a medium-size anatomical reference during socket development.

License: CC-BY-SA-4.0 — author must be credited, modified versions must carry the same license, commercial use allowed.

This work is based on "Beagle" (https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/beagle-341cb7dd930a427eab3f8e925718e6c0) by Gert-Jan van den Boom (https://sketchfab.com/alphafluff) licensed under CC-BY-SA-4.0 (http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/)

Software and Libraries¶

  • <model-viewer> by Google — the WebGL viewer behind every interactive 3D demo on this site. Apache License 2.0.
  • Draco by Google — mesh compression, which is what makes hundreds of models loadable in a browser. Apache License 2.0.
  • nTop — the implicit modeling platform used to generate every socket, lattice, and paw in the design demos.
  • trimesh and fast_simplification — used to decimate and prepare exported meshes for the web. MIT License.
  • Inter and Lato — typefaces served by Google Fonts under the SIL Open Font License.

Program Work¶

Unless credited above, the photographs, videos, CAD, socket geometry, lattice designs, and written documentation on this site were produced by Give a Paw students and mentors through the Canine Prosthetics DeCal at the University of California, Berkeley, run by Bioprinting@Berkeley.

Our thanks to Max, Billie, Yana, Ollie, and Kiki, and to the families and shelters who trusted us with their care.
