Mesh check & repair

Drop in a mesh and get a plain-English report of what is wrong with it, a 3D view that points at each problem, and a repaired file. It fixes the common things (split vertices, flipped and sliver triangles, small holes, inside-out shells, debris, overlapping bodies) and tells you exactly what it could not fix and why. Everything happens in your browser; nothing is uploaded. Need a web-ready GLB instead? Use the model converter.

Common mesh problems, and what they do to you

Almost every "nTop will not import my mesh", "Remesh fails", "the quad mesh looks like spaghetti" and "the print has gaps" comes down to one of these. The checker runs them in this order, because each one needs the earlier ones cleaned up first.

Split vertices & pinholes

Two triangles meet at a corner but store it twice, a hair apart. Looks closed, is not. Symptom: "mesh is not closed", zero-area triangles after remeshing. Cause: exports from CAD at loose tolerance, stitching surfaces by hand. Fix: weld vertices within a tolerance; done here automatically.

Degenerate & duplicate triangles

Triangles with zero area, or the same triangle twice. Symptom: normals go haywire, quad remesh fails, four triangles on one edge. Cause: Booleans, "double-sided" exports. Fix: delete them; done here.

Flipped triangles & inside-out shells

Normals pointing into the solid, for a patch or a whole shell. Symptom: dark or invisible patches, Boolean union subtracts the body, thickening goes inward, slicer prints it hollow. Cause: mirroring, negative scale, hand-edited faces. Fix: reorient to match neighbours and flip shells with negative volume; done here.

Holes & open edges

Edges used by only one triangle. Small loops are holes; long loops mean the surface was never closed. Symptom: Mesh to Implicit and Booleans refuse, the slicer fills the wrong side. Cause: scan gaps, surfaces that were never thickened. Fix: small holes are patched here; large openings need Thicken Mesh or a rebuild in CAD.

Non-manifold edges

An edge shared by three or more triangles (a fin or internal wall), or a bow-tie vertex. Symptom: "mesh is not manifold", Remesh and Booleans refuse to run. Cause: meshing two touching bodies as one, internal faces left after a Boolean. Fix: only by hand or by rebuilding: union the bodies implicitly before meshing; delete the fin in Meshmixer or Blender. Highlighted here, not changed.

Loose debris

Tiny separate shells floating in the file. Symptom: extra bodies, specks in the print, blobs in the implicit field. Cause: scanner noise, Boolean leftovers. Fix: delete pieces far smaller than the model; done here.

Sliver triangles

Needles (two corners nearly touching) and caps (a corner on the opposite edge). Symptom: remesh fails, quad conversion produces spikes, normals wrong. Cause: Booleans, decimation, tight STL tolerances. Fix: collapse needles, re-split caps with their neighbour; done here without moving the surface.

Self-intersections

Triangles passing through other triangles. The number-one reason a mesh "looks fine" but every downstream block fails. Two kinds: separate shells overlapping (socket + attachment exported together) and a single surface folding through itself (over-thickened, tight sweep). Fix: overlapping closed shells are Boolean-unioned here; a self-folding surface needs a coarser remesh, a smaller thickening distance, or an implicit rebuild.

Wrong units

Not a defect in the mesh, but the most common surprise: a 200 mm socket that arrives as 0.2 units was exported in metres (scanners, Blender). Symptom: nTop shows a speck or a building. Fix: the report flags suspicious sizes; set the unit on import in nTop or scale by 1000 in the converter.

Order matters: welding first turns most "non-manifold" and "open edge" reports into nothing, and winding has to be consistent before a shell can be called inside-out. The self-intersection sweep is the slow one (it compares neighbouring triangles on a grid) and stops at the time limit you choose; the report says how much of the model it covered. Repairs never move a vertex that was not already on top of another one, and never change units or orientation. For meshes it cannot fix, nTop's own route is usually best: Mesh to Implicit with a small tolerance, Boolean the bodies there, then Implicit to Mesh. Checks run with three.js for reading files and Manifold (WebAssembly) for the Boolean union.
